**Direct answer:** The live TV apps that feel premium from the couch share a few habits. Live video is one press away and never hidden behind a picture-in-picture step. A compact "now and next" layer sits over the playing video, so the full grid is rarely needed. Channels are shown by logo rather than plain text. Focus is shown by one strong change (scale plus a light border or glow on a calm dark surface) instead of several weak ones. When a programme has no picture, the tile falls back to the channel logo rather than an empty frame. Mobbin and Refero weren't available (no connector installed, and earlier runs hit paywalls), so everything below comes from web sources. I'm linking articles and galleries, not screenshots I captured myself.

Focus can use scale (default steps 1.025, 1.05 and 1.1), a glow of 2–32dp, an outline with an inset, and a colour change. The background stays still while surfaces take on a tint as they rise. [Android TV focus system](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/focus-system). Keep text inside safe margins of 48dp left and right and 27dp top and bottom. [Android TV layouts](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/tv/guides/styles/layouts). Use `focusRestorer` so each row remembers its focus. [Compose TV layouts](https://developer.android.com/training/tv/playback/compose/layouts)

**10 patterns worth copying for a dark, one-accent, D-pad-only app**
1. **OK on a channel plays it full screen right away, with no preview step (Sky OS 2.0).** Back from the guide returns to the video that's playing.
2. **Down during playback opens a one-row "now and next" strip of favourites, starting on the current channel (Channels Quick Guide).** OK switches channel and the video keeps playing. This replaces most trips to the full grid.
3. **Logos first, with a letter badge as fallback (Sky, Channels).** Since your logos are stored but not drawn, drawing them is the single biggest step up in polish. When a programme has no picture, use the channel logo on a flat tile tinted by the channel, never an empty grey box.
4. **Show time as a thin progress bar plus "ends 8:30" (YouTube TV shows time left).** Hide filler such as "Paid Programming" or untitled blocks from Home, and show it as plain text only in the grid.
5. **Keep Favourites and Recents at the far left of the guide, where focus lands first (Google TV).** Sky users complain because their Favourites sits far right.
6. **A sort or filter control anchored top left, always one press Up or Left away (Google TV, YouTube TV).** Pair it with a "Jump to now" action like YouTube TV's.
7. **Use one focus treatment everywhere:** scale 1.05 with a 2dp accent outline set a little outside the tile, on a static dark background (Android TV focus system). Don't use blur or full-screen glow, which matters for your roughly 21 ms frame budget. Show "selected" differently from "focused", for example with a filled accent dot or tick, so the two never look alike.
8. **Remember focus per row and per screen (Channels changelog, Compose `focusRestorer`).** Back returns to the exact tile you left.
9. **Push heavy setup off the TV (Google TV only allows custom channel order on phone or web).** For 48,000 channels, a "hide groups" list with language filters beats scrolling on the TV.
10. **Use big type and an option to turn off animation (Foxtel font complaints, Plex's guide-animation toggle).** Keep guide text at 10-foot sizes and give slow devices a reduced-motion setting.
